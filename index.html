Who owns the recalls

2012–2026 archive
87,010 listings rolled up

The ledgers above show who filed a recall. That is not the same as who owns the company. Philips files under 20 different names, Medtronic under 29, GE under 29 — so counting by filing name understates the biggest players by two to four times. We rolled all 9,054 filing entities up to their corporate parents, then joined each one to the federal contracts it holds.

60Corporate parents 7,662Recalls they filed 143FDA-mandated · 0.62% $149.6BFederal contracts held

Of 87,010 recalls filed since 2012, the FDA compelled 708. Everything else was voluntary. Meanwhile the companies filing the most are among the government’s larger medical suppliers — Pfizer alone holds $36.1B in federal contract obligations.

Your food. Their job.

Every action in the ledger above is a product that reached a shelf before anyone caught it. The data is public because the law says it has to be. Reading it is the easy part — here is what actually moves.

01

Get told first

The FDA emails recalls the day they're posted, days before most outlets pick them up. Free, and it covers everything, not just the stories that trend.

Subscribe at fda.gov ↗ 02

Report what you find

A great many recalls begin with one person reporting one bad package. If something made you sick or looked wrong, file it — or call 1-888-SAFEFOOD.

Report a problem to the FDA ↗ 03

Ask who's accountable

Inspection frequency, staffing and enforcement authority are set by Congress, not by the agency. The people who decide the budget work for you.
