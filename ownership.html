What the recalls did

FDA adverse event reporting · CAERS (food) and MAUDE (devices) · 1,000+ reports a month

—Citing hospitalization

—Recalls citing harm in the FDA's own record

A recall is filed because something reached a shelf. These are the reports of what happened to the people who bought it. The FDA runs two systems: CAERS for food, supplements and cosmetics, and MAUDE for medical devices. Both take reports from consumers, doctors and manufacturers.

Hospitalizations by product category

Supplements and cosmetics dominate — categories the FDA does not review before they go on sale.

Hospitalization reports per year

Reporting is voluntary for consumers, so these are a floor, not a count of harm.

Devices are a different scale

—Device injury reports
—Device death reports

MAUDE covers all medical devices, not only recalled ones. A report is not proof the device caused the outcome — but it is the record the FDA itself keeps.

Recalls where the FDA's own record cites illness, injury or death

696 distinct recalls, — of them Class I. These are the cases where harm is stated in the enforcement report itself — no inference required.

Sources. Adverse events: FDA CAERS (food, supplements, cosmetics) and MAUDE (medical devices), retrieved via openFDA. Both are voluntary reporting systems: a report is not proof the product caused the outcome, reports cannot be joined to a specific recall, and harm is widely under-reported. The recall-level harm figures come from the FDA's own enforcement text. Lobbying: U.S. Senate Lobbying Disclosure Act filings, 2023–2025, matched by client name. Recalls: openFDA enforcement reports (food, drug, device), June 2012 – August 2026, US federal public records. Federal contracts: USAspending.gov, contract obligations 2012–2026, matched by recipient name.

Method and limits. Parent mapping is a curated regex over normalized filing names and includes known acquisitions (Hospira→Pfizer, Merge→GE, Biomet→Zimmer, Bard→BD). It captures 60 parents covering 7,662 recalls; the remaining ~19,700 recalls belong to firms outside the mapping. Federal dollars are matched by name, not by UEI, so figures include all recipients whose name contains the parent string — treat them as order-of-magnitude, not audited. A recall is not evidence of wrongdoing, and holding a federal contract is not evidence of favouritism. Recalls are a cost to the companies that file them.
